Scope and priority legend
P0 means required for a controlled public pilot. P1 means the next product increment after the pilot is stable. P2 means a later capability retained in the roadmap. Priority controls delivery order; it does not remove a requested feature.
All performance and capacity figures in this document are proposed engineering targets. The audit must establish an agreed workload, available resources and acceptable impact on existing services before those targets become release commitments.
Decisions that override earlier suggestions
Use Python FastAPI for the proposed Careers service, subject to the repository audit, instead of adding a parallel Node backend. Start with PostgreSQL search and a database-backed work queue. Redis, BullMQ, n8n, Meilisearch, OpenSearch and vector search are optional future decisions, not initial dependencies.

6 Product scope and user journeys
Product outcomes
Help a visitor find relevant, current and credible vacancies quickly; help a member track progress and learn useful skills; help a verified employer publish opportunities; help administrators operate reliable sources and investigate abuse. Measure relevance, freshness, useful applications and successful delivery rather than artificial engagement.
Roles and journeys
Visitor: browse public jobs without registration; filter by place and work mode; see source, freshness and eligibility; follow the source's permitted application link. Public listings must not be hidden behind signup when a source forbids it.
Member: create a minimal profile, choose interests and location, separately enable profiling and alerts, save searches, link Telegram, receive a career brief and track applications. Consent refusal must leave ordinary search usable.
Employer: request an organisation account, prove domain ownership and undergo moderation, draft a job, preview it, accept posting terms and submit for review. Publication and candidate access require separate permissions.
Administrator: approve sources, inspect pipeline health, review employer and scam queues, handle takedowns, export operational evidence and control feature flags. Every privileged action is attributable.
Route map
/careers and /careers/jobs for discovery; /careers/jobs/:slug for details; /careers/companies and company pages; /careers/dashboard for personal recommendations and progress; /careers/applications; /careers/career-assistant; /careers/market-insights; /careers/employers; /careers/admin; /careers/settings/privacy and /careers/settings/alerts.
Feature delivery contract
P0: approved discovery, normalization, deduplication, fast search, city filters, evidence-based eligibility, source attribution, accounts, saved jobs/searches, transparent basic matching, Telegram digests, consent controls, scam reports and operational dashboard.
P1: optional radius search, richer recommendations, daily career brief, deadline radar, company following, application command centre, employer posting portal, CV readiness and skills demand views.
P2: advanced career assistant, interview practice, semantic matching where justified, richer labour analytics and employer analytics. Premium visual quality is a design standard throughout, not a promise that every screen launches at once.
Future monetisation may include clearly labelled sponsored jobs, optional career services and employer plans. [DECIDE: whether any paid product is intended]. Sponsorship must not alter safety moderation, imply verification or silently override relevance. Payment processing, taxes and refunds require a separate approved scope. Launch excludes bulk auto-apply, sale of personal profiles, recruitment decision automation and unrestricted third-party data resale.
 
7 Discovery and ingestion requirements
DISC-01 P0: Maintain an approved-source registry. Start with a small pilot of two or three independently approved sources, not every available endpoint. Expand by country, language, industry and employer coverage. Support official APIs, RSS/Atom, authorized sitemaps and employer pages. Global coverage is progressive; publish coverage gaps.
DISC-02 P0: Every adapter implements fetch, parse, normalize, validate and checkpoint. Enforce source-specific quotas, timeouts, response-size limits, identification, conditional requests, backoff with jitter and Retry-After. Disable an adapter on repeated forbidden responses, licence expiry or administrator action. Do not evade a provider's block.
DISC-03 P0: Preserve source ID, external ID, source URL, application URL, publication/update dates, first seen, last seen, last successful verification and ingestion timestamp. Missing source dates stay unknown; never use ingestion time as a fabricated posting date. Store raw payload only if permitted and only for its approved retention period.
DISC-04 P0: Normalize company, title, country, city, work mode, contract type, seniority, skills, language, salary currency/period, closing date, eligible countries and work-authorisation requirements. Keep original evidence and confidence separately from normalized fields. Do not infer salary, visa sponsorship or worldwide eligibility from absence of restrictions.
DISC-05 P0: Upsert by source and external ID. Link possible cross-source duplicates using employer identity, normalized title, location, requisition ID and content similarity. Keep source variants and provenance under a canonical job. Do not merge separate shifts or locations solely because titles match. Provide a moderator split/merge action with an audit trail.
DISC-06 P0: Publish only allowed fields after validation and moderation rules. Treat expired deadlines and explicit source closure as closure signals. A failed poll does not mean all jobs disappeared. Only mark absent jobs stale after successful complete snapshots and a configured grace period. Handle pagination and partial feeds before applying absence rules.
DISC-07 P0: Recheck application links safely. Record redirects and domain changes. A temporarily unreachable link is uncertain; a confirmed closed posting is inactive. Notify users of closure only when useful and supported. Remove closed jobs from active search and structured job data promptly.
DISC-08 P1: Discover new employer sources from user suggestions and approved directories. New domains enter a pending queue and cannot be fetched by ordinary workers until approved. HTML crawling requires terms and robots review, clear user-agent identification, per-domain pacing and permission evidence. Playwright is reserved for permitted dynamic pages; do not add stealth or proxy evasion.
Acceptance: repeating the same feed creates no duplicate source records; a partial or failed feed cannot mass-expire jobs; disabling a source halts new fetches and suppresses prohibited publication; every public job exposes its source and freshness.
 
8 Search and nearby jobs
SEARCH-01 P0: Search the local index rather than fetching external sites during a user request. Support title/keyword, country/city, remote/hybrid/on-site, eligible country, industry, job type, experience, salary currency/period, date and deadline filters. Preserve query state in shareable URLs without personal profile data.
SEARCH-02 P0: Start with weighted PostgreSQL full-text search for title, employer, skills and description; structured B-tree indexes for filters; optional pg_trgm for typo tolerance and suggestions after extension and performance review. Use language-aware indexing where supported. Start with English and preserve non-English source text; do not label translation as original employer wording.
SEARCH-03 P0: Rank with documented relevance, freshness and explicit user preferences. Clearly identify sponsored items. Provide stable cursor pagination and result count semantics. Deduplicate at canonical-job level. Empty states explain which filters removed results and let the user widen them deliberately.
LOC-01 P0: Ask for city or region first. Do not require GPS. Store country and city codes alongside the display name to distinguish places with the same name. Approximate city-centre distance is labelled approximate, not commute time.
LOC-02 P1: Optional browser geolocation is a separate user action. Use the location only for the stated purpose; retain rounded coordinates only if needed and consented. Use PostGIS geography and spatial indexes if installed and approved, otherwise start with city filters. A licensed offline place dataset can avoid paid maps; record its licence and attribution. Do not assume public geocoding endpoints permit bulk use.
LOC-03 P1: Radius values such as 5, 10, 25 and 50 km are editable defaults. Missing or low-confidence coordinates appear in a separate location-unknown group rather than falsely within the radius. Hybrid roles require the actual office location. Remote roles use eligibility filters, not proximity to headquarters.
Proposed performance targets
Search API p95 at or below 300 ms; suggestions p95 at or below 150 ms; verified job visible in the index within 60 seconds after publication transaction. Measure API latency separately from network and page rendering. Pilot benchmark proposal: 50,000 active jobs, 20 concurrent users and 10 search requests/second for 15 minutes, plus active ingestion. [DECIDE: workload after audit]. Report query plans, hardware, cold/warm runs and failure rates.
Keep public page caches short and invalidate on closure. Never cache private recommendations at Cloudflare as public content. Reuse the actual site rendering pipeline for indexable job pages, canonical URLs and sitemaps. Emit JobPosting structured data only where data is current, accurate and the source permits downstream distribution. Recheck search-engine requirements before release.
 
9 Consent and recommendations
REC-01 P0: Offer three modes: ordinary search with no persistent profile; short-lived session suggestions; and account-based personalization after explicit opt-in. Do not inspect searches on other websites, browser history, device traffic or other apps. A user can explicitly type interests without enabling behavioural history.
REC-02 P0: Consent controls separately cover search-interest learning, optional precise location, Telegram alerts and any external AI processing of personal documents. Record purpose, policy version, timestamp, interface and withdrawal. Do not use preselected profiling consent or make it a condition of browsing jobs. Apply local cookie/storage requirements to session technologies.
REC-03 P0: Treat searches, saved jobs, dismissed suggestions, company follows and application feedback as signals with clear purpose limits. Prefer explicit preferences over inferred ones. Decay old interests, cap event retention and allow the user to inspect, correct or reset the profile. Avoid inferring sensitive characteristics from search terms; redact or avoid storing such queries.
REC-04 P0: Hard-filter explicit ineligibility before scoring. Preserve unknown eligibility as a separate state. For a strict Ghana or Nigeria alert, include only explicit eligibility evidence for the selected country; let users separately opt into an uncertain-eligibility digest.
Initial explainable scoring proposal
Score known, relevant factors: skills 40%, experience 25%, location/work-mode preference 20%, salary preference 15%. Normalize weights over available factors and show evidence coverage, so a score based on one known factor is not presented as strong certainty. Missing salary is unknown, not zero salary. Scores express preference fit, not probability of being hired.
Show concise reasons such as “matches 4 of your 6 selected skills”, “remote eligibility explicitly includes Ghana” and “salary not stated”. Disclose required skills missing from the profile. Keep model/rule version, feature values and explanation for evaluation. Users can dismiss a reason or adjust a preference.
REC-05 P1: Add embeddings or LLM summaries only after a rules baseline, measured relevance improvement, approved model licence, data-processing review and resource budget. Cache job-only enrichment by content hash. Never send CVs to a provider without informed controls and an appropriate processing arrangement. AI suggestions remain editable.
Acceptance: declining consent produces no persistent interest events; withdrawal stops future learning and queued personalised alerts, deletes derived profiles under the deletion policy and leaves search available. Test eligibility negatives, missing data, multilingual titles and irrelevant recommendation feedback. Evaluate ranking with a human-labelled set across launch markets rather than optimising clicks alone.
 
10 Telegram alerts and Ghana Nigeria example
TG-01 P0: Create a bot under an owner-controlled account. Keep its token server-side. Link a signed-in TechTrendi account using a random, short-lived, single-use token stored as a hash. The user must start the bot and confirm linking. Do not put email addresses or database IDs in deep-link tokens. Prevent token replay and account-link takeover.
TG-02 P0: Support /start, /jobs, /remote, /saved, /alerts, /pause, /resume and /stop. Commands affecting private data require a valid account link. /stop revokes delivery immediately, removes pending work and confirms the setting; unlinking removes the association. Website controls provide equivalent access.
TG-03 P0: Offer immediate high-match alerts, daily and weekly digests, saved-search alerts, new followed-employer roles and deadline reminders. Default to one daily digest, a maximum of 10 jobs and quiet hours 21:00–08:00 in the user's timezone [DECIDE: defaults]. Give each message a clear source, location, eligibility state and link. Send only fields and links the source permits in notifications.
TG-04 P0: Use an outbox created in the same transaction as alert eligibility. Enforce a unique delivery key by user, canonical job, channel, alert type and digest period. Recheck consent, active job status, source rights and quiet hours at dispatch. Retry transient failures with bounded backoff; respect Telegram Retry-After; disable delivery to blocked accounts. Do not retry indefinitely.
Telegram supports free bot messaging within rate limits; its published guidance includes roughly 30 bulk messages per second and lower per-chat limits. Set conservative limits, avoid paid broadcast mode and recheck current documentation [R3]. A successful API response means Telegram accepted the message, not that the user read it. Record ambiguous timeout outcomes so retries do not falsely promise exactly-once delivery.

12 Employer portal and scam intelligence
EMP-01 P1: Organisation registration collects legal/display name, website, country, contact and verification evidence. Verify domain ownership and review impersonation risks. A matching email domain alone does not establish that a business or job is legitimate. Store verification status, evidence date, method and reviewer.
EMP-02 P1: Roles are organisation owner, recruiter and viewer; global moderation is separate. Invitations expire and can be revoked. Enforce membership on every write. Job workflow: draft, submitted, changes requested, approved, published, paused, closed, rejected. Moderation rules and appeal contact are visible.
EMP-03 P1: Validate salary currency/period, location, work mode, eligibility, application method, closing date and required qualifications. Prohibit application fees, misleading claims and discriminatory content under applicable rules. Employers attest that they have rights to submitted content. Edits to sensitive fields can trigger re-review.
EMP-04 P2: Employer analytics show their own aggregate views and outbound application clicks, with bot filtering and clear definitions. A click is not a completed application. Default application flow redirects to the employer. An on-platform applicant inbox is a separate opt-in scope requiring candidate sharing controls, retention rules and organisation isolation; employers never browse all member CVs.
SCAM-01 P0: Provide a report action on every job. Categories include upfront payment, impersonation, suspicious domain, unrealistic claims, phishing, expired listing and other. Rate-limit reports; allow evidence and a reference number. Keep reporter identities private.
SCAM-02 P0: Use explainable risk signals: payment requests, misleading domains, unexpected redirects, employer mismatch, unusual contact methods, duplicate spam and suspicious text. Signals create a review priority; they do not prove fraud. High-risk jobs may be quarantined pending review. A low score must not be presented as a guarantee of safety.
SCAM-03 P1: Job Scam Intelligence Centre publishes reviewed warnings with evidence and an appeal path. Use precise labels such as “reported”, “under review” or “confirmed by [evidence]”. Avoid unsubstantiated accusations. Track takedowns and notify affected users only with relevant, substantiated information.
Acceptance: an employer in organisation A cannot read or edit organisation B's drafts, candidates or analytics; unverified organisations cannot self-publish; reports cannot directly change public fraud labels; moderators can reverse a decision with an audit entry. Known malicious links are blocked from application redirects. [FILL: moderation owner, response targets, appeal contact and coverage hours].
 
13 Labour analytics and premium dashboards
AN-01 P1: Aggregate active unique vacancies, newly observed vacancies, closures, skills demand, industry mix, work modes, location coverage and salary ranges. Label the period, eligible source set, sample size, last refresh and methodology. Job advertisements are a sample of advertised demand, not total employment or official unemployment statistics.
AN-02 P1: Count canonical jobs once while preserving source coverage. Distinguish first observed from employer publication date. 14 Architecture and integration boundaries
Request and data flow
Browser → Cloudflare → nginx → existing React build for public pages.
Browser → nginx /api/careers/v1 → FastAPI → PostgreSQL and private storage.
Supabase Auth → validated user JWT → scoped user operations and tested RLS.
Approved source registry → scheduler → isolated fetch workers → validation and deduplication → publication transaction → search index and notification outbox.
Outbox → matching and dispatch workers → Telegram → delivery records.
Approved aggregates → public insights, personal dashboards and restricted admin metrics.
Minimal deployment units
Reuse the current frontend build. Add a Careers Python service, an ingestion worker and an alert worker as separate managed processes or containers matching the existing deployment pattern. A database queue avoids mandatory Redis. Use a single scheduler leader or database advisory lock; workers claim jobs atomically. Bind internal ports to loopback or a private container network.
Place Careers code and migrations in explicit modules or a careers schema after compatibility review. Expose only the intended Supabase schemas and endpoints. Do not couple ingestion to existing article generation jobs. Apply memory, CPU, connection, concurrency and disk budgets so source failures cannot exhaust the news platform.
FastAPI owns privileged orchestration. Where browser-to-Supabase access is retained, use the publishable/anonymous key with authenticated user JWTs and proven RLS, never a service-role key. Direct Python database connections must use scoped roles; RLS alone is ineffective if the role bypasses it. Validate JWT signatures, issuer, audience and expiry using the deployed Auth configuration.
Integration and deployment policy
Audit nginx precedence before adding /api/careers/. Preserve /api/ai/ and all existing routes. Add health and readiness endpoints with no secrets. Validate nginx configuration before reload. Use Cloudflare TLS to origin with valid certificates, strict public/private cache separation and the existing verified DNS model. Limit origin access where compatible with operational access and recovery.
CI performs type checks, build, unit/integration tests, migration checks, dependency/secret scans and a preview release. Pin dependencies and image versions. Use additive migrations, staged backfills and feature flags. Keep the prior release and migration compatibility window for rollback. Production deploys need an approved change record.
Optional scaling path: improve indexes and query plans first; introduce a dedicated search service only after benchmarks fail; add Redis or a queue service only when database queues become a proven bottleneck; enable vector search only when relevance gains justify resource use. PostgreSQL 15 and all reported versions require support/security review before launch; do not upgrade the shared database casually [R4].

Use /api/careers/v1 as the prefix. Generate an OpenAPI contract and TypeScript client types. Standard error body: code, safe message, request_id and optional field errors. Return 401/403 consistently, 429 with retry guidance, and never expose stack traces or SQL. Bound query complexity, upload size and page size. Use optimistic version checks for concurrent edits.
Queue and transaction semantics
Scheduler inserts a unique work item for a source and due slot. Workers atomically claim with a lease, for example FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED, commit the claim, then perform bounded external work outside the transaction. Renew leases for long jobs. On completion, checkpoint and record counts; expired leases permit recovery. Use retry limits and a dead-letter state visible to administrators.
Perform canonical record updates, permitted publication and outbox insertion transactionally. Match or dispatch asynchronously; never send Telegram while holding a database transaction open. Compare content versions so benign feed refreshes do not create duplicate new-job alerts. At-least-once worker execution requires idempotent writes and unique constraints.
Safe fetch and source state
Resolve and validate approved destinations, schemes and every redirect. Reject private, loopback, link-local, metadata and unexpected networks for both IPv4 and IPv6. Prevent DNS rebinding using validated resolution/connection controls and egress restrictions. Block arbitrary URLs from ordinary users from becoming fetch targets. Apply limits to decompression, content size, parser time and browser resources.
Acceptance: concurrent workers cannot claim the same active lease; crashing after a database commit does not duplicate job rows; repeated webhook updates do not repeat commands; pausing a source cancels its pending fetches; stale consent cancels outbox dispatch. Monitor oldest due work, retry counts and dead-letter age rather than queue length alone.
 
18 Security privacy and legal controls
Required security baseline
Separate ingestion, public API and admin privileges. Keep Supabase Studio and PostgreSQL off unrestricted public access. Rotate default secrets, require strong administrator authentication, protect deployment credentials and store secrets outside the frontend bundle and repository. Inventory subprocesses, open ports and dependencies before adding services.
Sanitize listing HTML and render safe text by default. Validate application links, enforce a restrictive content security policy and protect against XSS, injection, CSRF where cookie authentication is used, insecure object access and abusive uploads. Scan and isolate document parsing. AI tools must not execute instructions contained in listings, CVs or retrieved documents. Apply input/output limits and explicit tool permissions.
Audit privileged changes without recording tokens, CV text or raw sensitive searches. Encrypt transport and protect disks/backups according to the hosting capabilities. Test credential rotation and restore. Use resource limits and backpressure; pause lower-priority ingestion before degrading the existing news platform.
Privacy and legal launch gate
Identify the operating entity, launch jurisdictions and data flows before launch. Assess applicable GDPR, Ghanaian, Nigerian and other target-market rules, including cookies, international transfers, children, marketing and employment discrimination. A privacy/legal owner must validate lawful bases and notices for each purpose. The GDPR requires purpose limitation, minimisation and storage limits; profiling and significant automated decisions require particular assessment [R10]. Do not treat a consent checkbox as complete compliance.
Perform a privacy impact screening before behavioural profiling, precise location and CV AI processing. Record processors and transfer arrangements for hosting, Cloudflare, Telegram and any AI provider. Explain that Telegram is an external channel and send minimal personal data. Employer-side AI screening/ranking requires a separate assessment of applicable employment and AI rules before enablement; EU AI Act obligations depend on intended use and rollout [R11].
Proposed retention defaults for owner review
Session-only recommendations end with the session. Raw consented interest events: 30 days. Derived interests: reset on withdrawal and expire after 180 days of inactivity. Private CVs: user-controlled deletion plus an inactivity policy [FILL]. Alert logs: 90 days with minimal metadata. Security audit events: 180 days unless justified otherwise. Raw source payloads: shortest of approved licence period and 7 days. These are design defaults, not statutory retention rules.
Provide access, correction, export, deletion, consent withdrawal and appeal routes. Proposed deletion completion target: 30 days, subject to applicable legal deadlines and exceptions. Document backup expiry and prevent deleted data reappearing after restore. Maintain copyright/takedown and employer appeal procedures. Source permission, privacy compliance and security review are separate gates; passing one does not satisfy the others.
